# Relation to announced results

This comparison concerns results announced in the Superpermutators group by
24 October 2026. The coefficient proved here is
`926683/1814400 ≈ 0.510738`, smaller than the `1771/3456 ≈ 0.512442`
coefficient in [Halfar’s 6 October announcement](https://groups.google.com/g/superpermutators/c/6ozhk3faHDA).
Both developments use Pantone’s transport, completion, and word-assembly
construction. Halfar’s finite selections and block-replacement ideas are
credited in the paper and the [source notices](../NOTICE).

Several announced words are shorter than those stored here. The following
compares this release with Halfar’s
[October announcements](https://groups.google.com/g/superpermutators/c/iIgHJwUc9ww):

Differences are this release minus the announced value. The fourteen-symbol
entry here is an existence theorem; Halfar’s entry is an explicit recipe.
Our eleven-symbol word is shorter than the announced comparison. This is not
a claim that every stored word is a current record. The ten- and eleven-symbol
comparison values also appear in the [6 October announcement](https://groups.google.com/g/superpermutators/c/6ozhk3faHDA).

William Echols introduced compact fourteen-symbol recipes and improvements
obtained by reopening consecutive trails. Theo H. improved trail placement
and opening phase; Halfar combined these moves with his own selections and
searches. Their contributions are described in the
[October discussion](https://groups.google.com/g/superpermutators/c/iIgHJwUc9ww).

At eight symbols, Halfar announced 46,174 letters, followed by Theo’s 46,173
using the same selection with different openings and joins.
[That discussion](https://groups.google.com/g/superpermutators/c/a-oOgxxHHHg)
distinguishes the selection from its subsequent improvement. External word
checks are reported by their authors; they were not rerun for this release.
